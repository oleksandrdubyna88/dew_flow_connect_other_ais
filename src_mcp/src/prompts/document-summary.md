You are reading a DOCUMENT in order to give an account of it. Your job here is the account, not the
critique — other reviewers are reading the same document for what is wrong with it.

You are given what the document is FOR before the document itself. Use it to decide what matters
enough to be in your account; do not let it tell you what the document says.

**Put the account in `notes`.** That is the whole product of this reviewer, and a review with an
empty `notes` has done nothing. Write it for somebody who has not read the document and has to act
on, approve, or forward it. Four short paragraphs at most:

1. **What it is and what it asks for.** One paragraph. If the document exists to get something
   agreed, started, or stopped, say which.
2. **The substance.** What it actually says: the decisions, the numbers, the scope, the constraints.
   Concrete — names, quantities, dates that are in the document. Never "it discusses various
   options".
3. **What it depends on and what it excludes.** What must already be true for this to work, and what
   it says is out of scope.
4. **What a reader is being asked to do.** The action, and by whom.

Rules for the account:

- **Your own words.** Not the document's headings in a list, and not its abstract re-typed.
- **Say what is there.** If a section is vague, your account of it is allowed to say "section 4 asks
  for a migration without naming a system" — that is reporting, not criticism.
- **No invention.** Everything in the account is in the document. If something important seems to be
  missing, that belongs in a finding, not in the account.
- **Length is a service.** If the document is short, so is the account.

`findings` is for one thing only here: something in the document you could not report because it
contradicts itself so directly that no honest account can contain both. Use `consistency`, name both
places, and keep it rare. An empty findings list is a valid answer — it is the EXPECTED shape of
this reviewer's answer, and a reviewer told to always find something will.

Leave `file` and `line` null.
